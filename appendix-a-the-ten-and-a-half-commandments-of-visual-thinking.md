# Appendix A: The Ten (and a Half) Commandments of Visual Thinking

## 1. Solve Any Problem with a Picture

It doesn't matter what kind of problem we face.

If we can imagine it, we can draw it - and either way, we gain clarity.

## 2. No Longer Say, "I Can't Draw"

You were born a visual thinker.

Drawing skill is irrelevant - visual thinking is the point.

## 3. Avoid Drawing on the Linen

Always carry around a little notebook and a pen.

## 4. To Start, Draw a Circle and Give It a Name

The hardest line to draw is the first, so draw a circle, name it, and start.

## 5. Select the Best Picture Type from the "Basic Six" (Who/What, How Much, Where, When, How, Why)

Pick the framework that fits from [Chapter 7](07-frameworks-for-showing):

1. Who/What? → Portrait
2. How Much? → Chart
3. Where? → Map
4. When? → Timeline
5. How? → Flowchart
6. Why? → Multiple-Variable Plot

## 6. Anthropomorphise Everything

Crude faces and stick figures instantly grab attention and force understanding.

## 7. Take Advantage of Every Mental Trigger You Can (aka Use "Precognitive" Attributes)

Your brain processes visual cues before you notice them.

Use them - they free your mind up to think deeper.

## 8. Doodle Aloud - and Erase Even Louder

Describe your picture out loud as you draw it: what the pieces mean and why you're drawing them where you are.

Even stream-of-consciousness babble makes sense when it supports an emerging picture.

## 9. Don't Draw What's Out There, Draw What's In Here (aka The Sky Is Blue, Except When I Think It Isn't)

Draw what's in your head, not what's out there.

When your problem is on paper, the solution jumps off the page.

## 10. Draw a Conclusion

Push your picture until something new emerges.

When you're done, write a title, conclusion, and insight/comment.

## 10.5. Don't Lie (Not To Yourself, Not to Your Audience, and Most of All Not to Your Picture)

The wrong picture is worse than no picture.

Review yours when it's done and make sure you haven't fooled yourself.
