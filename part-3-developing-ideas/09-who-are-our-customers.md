# 9. Who Are Our Customers?

> **"Framework 1: To show a who/what problem, use a portrait."**

## The Customer Crisis

Businesses often don't know exactly who their customers are.

Before talking to customers, they need a clear portrait of _who_ they're seeking - like a UI/UX User Persona.

### Portraits: General Rules of Thumb

1. **Think Simple.**
   Make a simple diagram identifying the _who_, not a work of art.
2. **Illuminate Lists.**
   Drawing portraits can spark qualitative ideas that writing lists miss.
3. **Visually Describe.**
   Pictures beat words for fast comparison - seeing eases comparing.

### Assessing Against SQVID

1. **Simple or Elaborate?**
   First attempts should stay simple.
2. **Qualitative or Quantitative?**
   It's a portrait, so it'll be qualitative by default.
3. **Vision or Execution?**
   n/a - you're not choosing a goal or a path.
4. **Individual or Comparison?**
   It'll be a comparison across your whole range of customers.
5. **Delta (Change) or Status Quo?**
   It's a baseline so it'll be status quo by default.

> "Before thinking too hard, it's helpful to know that although the first mark on the napkin is the most difficult to make, it is also among the least important."
>
> "A good way to start _any_ picture is to draw a circle and give it a name."

Pictures alone can keep our visual centres happy.

Other mental processing areas need written names/labels, or they'll invent them.

- These invented labels invite misinterpretation.
- Label **everything** to remove doubt.

You also need a clear title of what you just drew.

> "So as a rule, spell it out right on the top, every time."
