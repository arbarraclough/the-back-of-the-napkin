# 10. How Many Are Buying?

## Charts: General Rules of Thumb

1. **It's the data that matters, so let it show.**
2. **Pick the simplest model to make your point.**

    There are actually only four types of chart:

    1. Bars: comparing absolute quantities of something.
    2. Lines & Areas: comparing absolute quantities between two different criteria or times.
    3. Pies: comparing relative quantities of something.
    4. Bubbles: comparing more than two variables.

3. **If you start with one model, stay with one model.**

    If your chart has the right coordinate system to convey the data, it should be easy for the audience to read.

    This "primes" their mind to read that kind of data/chart, don't undo that by flipping an axis, switch chart types, or introduce a new mental model.

    - Think of charts as a drive through a scenic landscape: you want gentle/expected transitions.

## Pie Fight

There are two distinct camps when it comes to pie charts:

1. Pie charts are easy to create, visually pleasing, and easy to read.
2. Our eyes are poor at judging proportional slice sizes; straight verticals/horizontals are easier to compare.

A stacked percentage chart shows the same data with straight lines instead.

> "If the differences among slices are so critical yet so small as to be difficult to visually detect, you're better of going back to a non-pictorial table anyway."
