# 12. When Can We Fix Things?

> **"Framework 4: To show a when problem, use a timeline."**

## Timelines: General Rules of Thumb

1. **Time is a one-way street.**

    To make any timeline easily-readable, make sure that it's always going forward in time from left-to-right; that's the standard everyone can recognise and agree on.

2. **Repeating timelines create life cycles.**

    Timelines often repeat. Such timelines are called "lifecycles" and are typically represented as an endless circle or with an arrow returning to the start. If you can't identify a starting point, pick a major milestone anywhere along the length of the cycle and start there.

3. **Round versus linear.**

    While circular timelines are a more accurate representation of a repeating lifecycle, it's almost-always better to go with a straight line; they're easier to draw, easier to read, and easier to remember.

### Timeseries

A timeseries chart is a hybrid framework that combines a _how much_ chart with a _when_ timeline.

- Like in any timeline, the x-axis represents time and, like in any other chart, the y-axis represents amount.
