# 4. No Thanks, Just Looking

People don't draw because they think "I can't draw", flip that script.

Being able to draw well is an outcome of being able to see well, and being able to see well is an outcome of being able to look well.

There are four _looking_ tasks that your eyes do automatically for you, and that you need to progress:

1. Orientation
2. Position
3. Identification
4. Direction

## How to Look Better: Four Rules to Live By

To develop good looking skills, and build a good foundation for visual thinking, apply these basic rules:

1. Collect everything we can to look at; the more the better.
2. Have a place where we can lay our everything and really look at it all, side by side.
3. Always define a basic coordinate system to give us a clear orientation and position.
4. Find ways to cut ruthlessly from everything our eyes bring in; a visual triage.

## Looking Rule 1: Collect Everything Possible Up Front

When you start collecting, you're faced with one of two problems, either:

1. You've collected too much, or
2. You've collected too little.

Regardless of which situation you're in, the process is the same: look at what you've collected and apply _active looking_.

- Take the tasks that your eyes do automatically, and put conscious effort towards them.

## Looking Rule 2: Lay It All Out Where You Can Look At It

When data is packed away, it's impossible to look at the big picture.

Getting everything out in the open makes otherwise invisible connections visible, and allows people's innate creativity to thrive.

> "In the end, the final design emerged not from a formal review, but from the perceptive comments of an accountant who couldn't stop looking at two of the drawings."

## Looking Rule 3: Establish the Underlying Information Coordinates

Our eyes naturally visualise things according to three dimensions that form a coordinate system: length, height, and depth.

To make that translate to business problems, we can change the coordinate system according to the 6 W's.

1. Who/What
2. How Much
3. Where
4. When
5. How
6. Why

Every visualisation you make plots one of those six against another to form coordinates.

## Looking Rule 4: Practice Visual Triage

Senior people are able to quickly determine what does/doesn't need attention based on intuition.

This "intuition" comes from many "low-level" cognitive processes that your eyes do automatically, called "precognitive cues".

- **Proximity:** our eyes assume that things closer together are related.
- **Colour:** our eyes immediately notice differences in colour and assume groupings from them.
- **Size:** our eyes see size differences with zero effort; the odd one out is the one worth noting.
- **Orientation:** our eyes instantly distinguish between vertical and horizontal.
- **Direction:** our eyes can "see" perceived movement very quickly.
- **Shape:** our eyes notices differences in shape, though less strongly.
- **Shading:** our eyes quickly detect differences in shading as a way to determine up and down, or in and out.
