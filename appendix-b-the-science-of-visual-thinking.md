# Appendix B: The Science of Visual Thinking

## How We See, Part 1: The Vision Pathways

In [Chapter 4](04-no-thanks-just-looking), Roam described how light enters our eyes and gets converted to electrical signals passed to different brain regions for processing into the pictures we _see_.

It's an accurate and useful summary of the basics of our visual system, but it barely scratches the surface.

> "Every second that our eyes are open, millions of visual signals enter as photons of light, are instantly converted into electrical impulses by our retinas, and then get passed along through the million strands of our optic nerves into our brains."

After the left- and right-side signals are merged in the optic chiasma, about 10% of the signals are passed through the "old pathway" and the remaining 90% goes through the "new pathway".

### The Old Pathway

The brain stem, also known as the "reptilian brain", is responsible for our basic "fight or flight" survival skills.

This pathway only tells us one thing: _where_ stuff is.

- It doesn't provide any information about _what_ we're seeing, or even identify anything by name.

All it does is tell us if we're upright and if something is coming towards us.

### The New Pathway

The _lateral geniculate nucleus_ (LGN) is our central "visual triage station" that sits across the front of both left and right hemispheres of the neocortex.

- The neocortex is the part of the brain responsible for conscious thought, analytic decision making, naming, high-level processing, and emotion.
- After initial categorisation in the LGN, the signals get broken down into two other pathways: the _what_ pathway (where objects are recognised and identified) and the _new where_ pathway (where more detailed positional information is processed).
- After that, nobody really knows what happens next.

## How We See, Part 2: Right Brain Versus Left Brain

The left hemisphere is _analytic_, piecing together small bits of data into linear, rational thoughts.

- The left side contains the brain centres responsible for both written and verbal language and most mathematical calculation.

The right hemisphere is _synthetic_.

Processing large and less well-defined blocks of information through imagery, pattern, and spatial orientation.

- The right side is better at addressing complexity and ambiguity.

It appears to house the brain's creative centres.

## How We See, Part 3: The Things We Don't Know

Reviewing textbooks on vision science and speaking with professors of neurology always lead to the same point: We have only begun to scratch the surface on understanding how vision works.
