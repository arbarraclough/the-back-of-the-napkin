# Appendix C: Resources for Visual Thinkers

## Software

Notebooks, napkins, and whiteboards should still be the tools of choice for those looking to improve their innate visual thinking skills, however, some software Roam recommends are:

1. **Portraits:** Microsoft PowerPoint, Apple Keynote, Adobe Illustrator
2. **Charts:** Microsoft Excel, Microsoft PowerPoint, Apple Keynote, Adobe Illustrator
3. **Maps:** Mindjet, Microsoft PowerPoint, Apple Keynote, Microsoft Visio, Adobe Illustrator
4. **Timelines:** Microsoft PowerPoint, Microsoft Project, Graphus
5. **Flowcharts:** Mindjet, Microsoft Visio
6. **Multiple-Variable Plots:** Microsoft PowerPoint, Apple Keynote, Adobe Illustrator

## Books

Books Roam found inspiring and insightful while developing his ideas are:

### Creative Problem Solving

1. Buzan, Tony. _The Mind Map Book: How to Use Radiant Thinking to Maximize Your Brain's Untapped Potential._ New York: Plume, 1996.
2. Degani, Asaf. _Taming HAL: Designing Interfaces Beyond 2001._ New York: Palgrave, 2004.
3. Gelb, Michael J. _How to Think Like Leonardo da Vinci: Seven Steps to Genius Every Day._ New York: Delacorte, 1998.
4. Grandin, Temple. _Thinking in Pictures: My Life with Autism._ New York: Vintage, 2006.
5. Kelley, Tom. _The Art of Innovation._ New York: Doubleday, 2000.
6. Root-Bernstein, Robert and Michele. _Sparks of Genius: The 13 Thinking Tools of the World's Most Creative People._ New York: Mariner Books, 1999.
7. Sawyer, R. Keith. _Explaining Creativity: The Science of Human Innovation._ Oxford: Oxford University Press, 2006.
8. Stafford, Tom, and Matt Webb. _Mind Hacks: Tips & Tools for Using Your Brain._ Sebastopol, CA: O'Reilly, 2005.
9. Thorpe, Scott. _How to Think Like Einstein: Simple Ways to Break the Rules and Discover Your Hidden Genius._ Naperville, IL: Sourcebooks, 2000.
10. Von Oech, Roger. _A Whack on the Side of the Head: How You Can Be More Creative._ New York: Warner Books, 1983.

### Neurobiology and Vision Science

1. Chalupa, Leo M., and John S. Werner. _The Visual Neurosciences._ Cambridge, MA: MIT Press, 2004.
2. Hawkins, Jeff, with Sandra Blakeslee. _On Intelligence._ New York: Times Books, 2004.
3. Palmer, Stephen E. _Vision Science: Photons to Phenomenology._ Cambridge, MA: MIT Press, 1999.
4. Ramachandran, V. S., and Sandra Blakeslee. _Phantoms in the Brain: Probing the Mysteries of the Human Mind._ New York: Harper Perennial, 1999.

### Visual Exercises and Insights for Non-Artists (and Artists, Too, Of Course!)

1. Arnheim, Rudolf. _Visual Thinking._ Berkeley: University of California Press, 1969.
2. DiSpezio, Michael A. _Visual Thinking Puzzles._ New York: Sterling, 1998.
3. Edwards, Betty. _The New Drawing on the Right Side of the Brain._ New York: Jeremy P. Tarcher, 1979.
4. Few, Stephen. _Show Me the Numbers: Designing Tables and Graphs to Enlighten._ Oakland, CA: Analytics Press, 2004.
5. Tufte, Edward R. _The Visual Display of Quantitative Information._ Cheshire, CT: Graphics Press, 1983.
6. Wainer, Howard. _Graphic Discovery: A Trout in the Milk and Other Visual Adventures._ Princeton, NJ: Princeton University Press, 2004.
7. Zelazny, Gene. _Say It with Charts: The Executive's Guide to Visual Communication._ New York: McGraw-Hill, 2001.
